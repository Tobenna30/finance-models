## What happened

My pre-results estimate grew each of LSEG's 14 income lines at its H1 2026 rate. Q3 came in 1.4% above that. Markets beat by 1.7%, led by Fixed Income, Derivatives & Other (+£11m vs my estimate), OTC derivatives (+£5m) and net treasury income (+£5m). FTSE Russell subscriptions beat by 4.0% and Risk Intelligence by 2.9%. Data & Analytics was in line (+0.6%). Equities and non-cash collateral were below.

Guidance went up on every line: organic income growth of at least 8% (from 7% to 7.5%), a margin gain of 100 to 120bp (from about 100bp), and equity free cash flow of at least £2.8bn (from £2.7bn).

## Octus

LSEG agreed to buy Octus for $3.535bn in cash, funded with debt. Management says it is neutral to adjusted EPS in the first full year and accretive after that. With my assumptions (40% EBITDA margin, 12% revenue growth, 5% cost of the new debt) it is roughly neutral in 2027, but my DCF values it below the price paid: it takes about 234p off the value per share. To be neutral on 2028 EPS, Octus would need an EBITDA margin of about 56%. Its margin is not disclosed, and it is the question I would ask at the full-year results.

## What it means for the view

The quarter supports the operating case: subscription lines held up and Markets kept growing off a high base. The deal is the new question. If Octus margins are well above 40%, or the debt costs less than 5%, the value comes back. If not, LSEG has paid about 28 times this year's EBITDA for it, at a 40% margin.
