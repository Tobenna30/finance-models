# Darktrace: was 620p a fair price?
*Project 04, sell-side view. Thoma Bravo's recommended cash offer for Darktrace plc: announced 26 Apr 2024, effective 1 Oct 2024. Model: models/Darktrace_04_08.xlsx. Figures in $ unless stated; per-share values in pence at the announcement rate of $1.25. Not investment advice.*

## Verdict
The offer was fair but not full. At 620p ($7.75) Thoma Bravo paid 20% over the undisturbed close of 517p and 44% over the three-month VWAP, or 8.1x LTM revenue and 34x LTM adjusted EBITDA. That is above my standalone DCF (455p, or 551p if share-based pay is ignored) and inside the range a sponsor could pay for a 20 to 25% IRR (593p to 661p). It is well below what listed US peers and the 2019 to 2022 take-privates imply (817p and 879p at the medians). The board's recommendation was defensible because the market had not offered Darktrace shareholders those multiples since 2022, and no rival had a reason to pay more.

The two premiums tell different stories. The shares had already risen 20% from the three-month VWAP to the undisturbed close, helped by the March 2024 placing clearing and an upgraded FY24 guide in the half-year results. Measured against the last close, 20% is low for a take-private. Measured against the three-month average, 44% is in line with precedents.

**DCF.** Revenue growth fades from 21% in FY25 to 9% in FY31 as net ARR added has stalled at about $145m a year. Adjusted EBITDA margin rises from 24% to 31%, consistent with the company's mid-20s steady-state adjusted EBIT target plus D&A of about 4% of revenue. Share-based pay, 6.9% of revenue in H1 FY24, is treated as a cost and falls to 4%. Cost of equity is 10.8% (US 10-year 4.54%, beta 1.25, ERP 5%) and terminal growth 3.5%. The result is 455p, with 70% of value in the terminal year at an implied 9.0x terminal EBITDA. Adding share-based pay back lifts value to 551p, still below the offer.

**Trading comps.** Seven US-listed security peers traded at a median 8.2x NTM revenue on 19 Apr 2024. The offer was 6.2x. The peer range is wide (Rapid7 3.8x to CrowdStrike 17.0x) and growth explains much of it: a simple regression of multiple on guided growth gives 9.3x for Darktrace's 21%. On that basis the offer looks cheap. The counter is that Darktrace had not traded near peer multiples since 2022. Its January 2023 short-seller episode, a share-based pay bill above most peers, and flat net ARR additions kept a discount in place that a buyer did not have to pay away.

**Precedents.** Seven cyber take-privates from Sophos (2019) to KnowBe4 (2022) cost a median 11.7x last fiscal-year revenue and a median 44% premium. Five of the seven were struck when the US 10-year yield was below 3%. With the yield at 4.5% in April 2024, paying 8.1x LTM revenue looks consistent with a lower multiple regime, not a discount to it. Sophos, the closest UK comparable and also a Thoma Bravo deal, went at 5.6x.

**LBO.** On my operating plan and the actual debt package, a sponsor clears 20% at 661p and 25% at 593p. Thoma Bravo paid in the middle of that range, which is what a disciplined sponsor does.

## Board defence case
A board wanting to hold out for more had four arguments.

- **Peer multiples.** At the peer median the shares are worth 817p, and the growth-adjusted view is higher. This is the strongest argument and the one the board itself used when it described the shares as trading at a significant discount to global peers.
- **Prior rejections.** The board had already turned down earlier unsolicited proposals from Thoma Bravo as not reflecting value, so the 620p price was the product of negotiation.
- **Momentum.** In H1 FY24 revenue grew 27.4%, ARR 24.4% and the adjusted EBITDA margin was 25.6% against a full-year guide of at least 21%. The July 2024 trading update later confirmed FY24 growth of at least 26% and net ARR retention of 106.6%.
- **Alternatives.** A US listing or a larger buyback could narrow the discount without a sale.

None of these was decisive. The intrinsic value on reasonable assumptions sits below 620p, so shareholders were being asked to wait for a re-rating that had not come since the shares peaked in 2021. The main holders, KKR DA and Summit Partners, signed irrevocables for 11.3%. Jefferies and Qatalyst advised the board that the terms were fair and reasonable. Shareholders agreed: 99.6% of Scheme Shares voted were in favour.

## Would a rival bid make sense?
Probably not.

- **A strategic buyer** (for example a large US security platform) could add cost synergies of about 10% of FY25 revenue, worth $542m after tax and integration costs. Paying away half on top of my DCF gives 487p, below the offer. A strategic would only beat 620p if it valued Darktrace on peer multiples, which its own shareholders would question for a business with slowing net new ARR.
- **A rival sponsor** using the same plan and debt reaches 661p at a 20% IRR, 7% above the offer. That headroom is thin against the cost of a contested bid, the need to match a debt package of 14.7x LTM EBITDA, and 14.7% of the shares already committed to Thoma Bravo under irrevocables that stay binding against a higher offer.
- **The market test.** The offer period ran five months, from announcement to the court sanction on 24 Sep 2024, and no competing bid appeared.

## What would change this view
A higher FY24 margin (I assume 23% against a guide of at least 21%) or a lower cost of equity would push the DCF towards the offer: at a 9.8% WACC and 4% terminal growth the DCF reaches 561p. A buyer who believed growth would hold above 20% for longer could justify paying peer multiples. The model's Inputs sheet holds each of these as a single cell.
